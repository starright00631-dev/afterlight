# Afterlight 3.4: Reading Room and shared-work demonstration

Show one investigation through **Arrange → Predict → Compare → Choose**. These controls open real editing, prediction, saved-pair comparison and decision work. Keep the visitor's interaction central; describe the ideal model before presenting a calculation as evidence.

## Prepare

Run Start-Afterlight.bat and confirm the application is ready at http://127.0.0.1:5180/. Use the current release QA report for verified platform and provider claims. Demonstrate without AI unless a provider has actually completed a live request.

Prepare an explicitly labelled example study: a 2.4 × 1.2 m shared table with point A at (0.8, 0.6) and B at (1.6, 0.6). Set each point's personal target to 150–200 lux. Save arrangement A with one 60 cd lamp above A, at 0.5 m height. Save arrangement B with two 40 cd lamps above A and B, each at 0.5 m. Do not add fabricated experiment or observation records. Leave the decision unset so that it can be retained live.

The model gives A approximately 240.0 and 35.7 lux; B approximately 183.8 lux at both points. **B minus A is −56.2 lx at the left point and +148.1 lx at the right point.** These are deterministic calculations for this geometry, not room measurements. If using another layout, use its actual report values instead.

## 0:00–0:35 — Arrange around a useful question

“Two people share this table. Can I light both work points within my chosen range, and retain why I chose that arrangement?”

Use **Arrange** to reach the editable working layout and labelled controls. Show the named points and personal targets. Switch from **Room** to **Light field**, then **Paths**. The field exposes distribution; paths explain an individual contribution. Controls and readings belong to the same model. Day/night changes the appearance while preserving the study and calculations.

The furnished room and wood material use three original generated bitmaps: room-night.png, room-day.png and table-oak.png. Lamps, screens, points, fields and numerical labels remain live. Decorative daylight, books and furniture do not contribute calculated light or shadow.

## 0:35–1:20 — Let a prediction meet a result

Open the distance commission. Its initial 32 cd lamp is 0.8 m directly above the detector, yielding 50 lux. Use **Predict**, select brighter, and write: “With intensity fixed, halving height should quadruple this reading.” **Commit prediction**, set height to 0.4 m and **Run experiment**. The calculated reading is 200 lux. Proposed readings remain covered until Run; the frozen baseline remains inspectable.

Invite the judge to change height or inspect the light path. Explain that directly under this ideal point lamp E = I / h²; elsewhere distance and incidence angle both matter. Write a separate conclusion and **Finalize experiment**. Show the preserved original prediction and both scenes. The process records a test; it does not assign mastery from a correct button press.

## 1:20–2:10 — Compare arrangements and keep a reason

Return to the prepared shared-table study. Use **Compare** and explicitly select saved A and B on the same surface and point coordinates. Open **Difference**: it shows calculated **B minus A**, with blue meaning less light and amber meaning more light. The signed lux legend is symmetric around zero. Show −56.2 lx at the left point and +148.1 lx at the right point alongside the A/B numbers. Swapping A and B reverses both signs; two separately saved identical scenes give a neutral zero field. The view uses retained scenes and is read-only. Later working edits cannot rewrite it, and different measurement setups are rejected.

A leaves the second point below the chosen target; B brings both to approximately 183.8 lux. Point uniformity becomes 100% for these two chosen points. The candela settings total 60 for A and 80 for B; this is not a watts or energy comparison. Inspect each lamp's contribution and target margins if the visitor wants the numerical explanation.

Use **Choose** and retain B. Record a reason tied to the point evidence, then a next check: measure both actual work points with an identified instrument while noting ambient light and fixture differences. Link an actual retained source passage if available. The decision freezes its comparison and target revision; later edits do not rewrite it. If no matched pair is saved yet, the action explains that prerequisite rather than creating a decision.

## 2:10–3:00 — Take the evidence into work

Choose **Export study → Download report (.html)**, the verified report-download path. Show configuration, results, target margins, original prediction, conclusion, decision and scientific boundary together. Mention **Readings for Excel (.csv)** for numbers and **Save study (.json)** for the configuration. Full source content requires **Backups and examples → Save backup (.json)**. Printing the downloaded HTML from a normal browser remains a manual acceptance check; do not claim a printed PDF has been verified.

Show **Check with a real reading** as an optional next step. Enter only an actual measurement with its instrument, time and context; leave the example's observations empty during this demonstration. Calculated and measured values stay distinct.

Use the retained decision in source-linked work or schedule the concrete measurement check. Week and Quiet support finishing and returning to this investigation. End by giving the judge control of a lamp, point or screen so the interactive core can be tried directly.

## Disclosure and closest comparisons
